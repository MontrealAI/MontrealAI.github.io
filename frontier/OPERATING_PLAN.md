# MONTREAL.AI — Frontier Mandate Ω
## One buyer-facing front door; one falsifiable commercial experiment

**Status: proposed commercial experiment, not a sold or deployed service.** This PR introduces a bilingual, privacy-preserving mission intake and a concrete standard for qualifying the first paid engagement. It does not prove demand, revenue, model autonomy, or market leadership.

### Strategic objective
Become independently recognized for discovering *newly attainable, consequential outcomes* and demonstrating real incremental value through a connected series of production missions. Do **not** attempt to compete with frontier labs on model training scale. Compete on verified realized outcome, newly constructed productive capability, causal successor advantage, and economic sustainability.

### The customer offer
"Bring a result that matters, especially one your current tools, budgets or processes cannot reach. We will investigate the strongest feasible route, including existing tools and providers, then propose an evidence-gated construction and acceptance plan."

No fixed delivery time, result, price or security guarantee is promised by the site. Each signed proposal needs a specific outcome, authorized scope, responsibilities, cost and acceptance agreement.

### Eight mechanisms — practical commercial use
1. **Structural transfer:** look for an existing mechanism in another discipline that solves the same structural constraint.
2. **Adversarial discovery:** disconfirm the buyer's diagnosis, ours, and popular vendor narratives.
3. **Strategic invention:** produce at least three *materially distinct* feasible routes (best incumbent, different strategy, changed constraints).
4. **Problem transformation:** explicitly seek to eliminate the expensive dependency rather than optimize around it.
5. **Epistemic invention:** preregister the instrument that would make an inaccessible claim testable.
6. **Self-correcting evidence:** independent reviewer and a route for failed or disputed findings.
7. **Generative inheritance:** retain only rights-cleared, tested productive assets; test advantage on fresh successor work.
8. **Recursive economic selection:** rank realized complete-cost value and finance the next advance only from legitimately available resources.

### Market experiment (prospective; not results)
- **Audience:** executives with authority over a material process and ownership of the acceptance metric.
- **First wedge:** expensive, repeatable, measurable knowledge-production processes where error review is already standard, and where integration is reversible. Example candidates: industrial proposal generation, procurement analysis, technical document production.
- **Distribution:** public MONTREAL.AI site; consent-based participation by interested event attendees; authorized direct outreach by a responsible commercial representative. Do not infer permission to contact historical event participants.
- **Starting sample:** recruit 20 well-matched conversations, prospectively record source and buyer authority, without treating the number as a market-size estimate.
- **Demand gate:** seek at least 5 qualified buyer problems and 1 fully authorized *paid* diagnostic/pilot. These are proposed decision thresholds, not validated conversion benchmarks.
- **Technical gate:** accepted deliverable on a buyer-defined measure with independent or buyer-designated review, within declared cost/risk limits.
- **Inheritance gate:** a fresh second mission with a matched capable control and explicit incremental value net of reuse/maintenance cost.
- **Economic gate:** real collected revenue minus *all* attributable costs, liabilities and reserves; no synthetic ledger or financing assumption counted as earned profit.
- **Founder-dependency gate:** repeat ordinary intake, construction and acceptance under qualified delegated operators; keep reserved matters with governing authority.

### Evidence ledger — minimum fields
date; channel; anonymous lead key; buyer role; problem category; signed authorization (yes/no); alternative providers; baseline; objective and acceptance; quote; invoiced; collected; acquisition costs; delivery costs; verification costs; support provision; customer verdict; appeal; reuse rights; successor test; independent reviewer; required founder decisions.

**Privacy:** do not commit identifiable leads, correspondence, proprietary data or secrets to the public repository. Keep customer-specific ledgers in approved private systems. Publish only consented, aggregated or appropriately anonymized proof.

### Decision discipline
1. If no real buyer has a consequential, fundable objective, test a different segment or proposition rather than expand the framework.
2. If an incumbent already delivers within the same budget and rights, recommend that alternative; do not manufacture an invention.
3. If the first mission succeeds but the second receives no advantage, sell a useful service if economics work but do not claim an invention flywheel.
4. If it is profitable but dependent on the president for every routine decision, build delegation before claiming institutional independence.
5. If independently verified and replicable, increase commercial capacity and request approval for any capital deployment outside established authority.

### Implementation notes
- Entry: `/frontier/` and `/frontier/fr/`.
- `mission.js` creates a deterministic, browser-local planning brief, not an LLM response. It does not fetch or store user content.
- No form submissions, third-party trackers, wallet connections or account signup.
- Email link requires a deliberate user click and uses the published `info@montreal.ai` address.
- Home CTA routes to the new bilingual intake; the final email dispatch is still a separate, deliberate user act.
- Site merge/deployment remains a human decision; opening a draft PR is not publication.
