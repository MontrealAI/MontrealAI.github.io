# Claims and Boundaries

## Governing distinctions

- **Execute:** produce bounded work inside granted permissions.
- **Validate:** challenge claims and assess evidence.
- **Accept:** a responsible customer or authority decides what may be relied upon.
- **Admit:** Chronicle decides what may enter governed institutional memory.
- **Settle:** value moves only through the applicable transaction and authorization gate.

These acts are intentionally separate. A model cannot accept itself. A validator does not automatically become the customer. Chronicle admission is not equivalent to payment. A blockchain commitment can prove what was recorded or signed; it cannot independently prove that an off-chain event was true.

## Public-site boundary

The parent site is a static public information and demonstration estate. It contains no first-party analytics, account system, checkout, custody, wallet transaction request, token sale or server-side private-evidence intake. Illustrative artwork is not evidence of a physical facility or deployment. The real supplied founder image is used; no synthetic likeness is substituted.

## Commercial and financial boundary

Illustrative management scenarios, probability-weighted examples, product theses and operating models are not realized customer outcomes, forecasts, securities offerings, financial advice or guaranteed returns. Customer acceptance and real economics must be established by external evidence.

## Recursive-improvement boundary

A new generation earns its name only through equal or harder constraints, fresh-task transfer and external or independent challenge. The institution is never its own final judge. Failed work remains visible and may be repaired, narrowed, quarantined, rejected or stopped.
